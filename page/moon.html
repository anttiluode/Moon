CrystalMoon, lit by written weights

A moon lit by a network nobody trained

Light scatters dozens of times inside this quasicrystal before it leaves. A path tracer needs millions of light paths per image to get that right. Here a small network does it per frame, and every one of its weights was written by hand from radiative-transfer formulas, then compiled into the shader you are looking at.

Read the network, switch parts of it off

The readout layer adds a handful of named terms. Unticking one sets its readout weight to zero and recompiles: everything that only fed that term drops out of the shader. With only the first box ticked you get plain single scattering, the usual cheap baseline.

  • Verified. The builder script reproduces the weight file byte for byte. A generic interpreter running the weights matches the formulas to 5e-8. The held-back views were scored after the design was frozen, once with the interpreter and once with the compiled shader of this page in headless Chromium.
    • 
    
  • Hand-set, not derived. Two constants, BETA = 0.6 and LAM = 0.7, were picked by looking at the 12 reference views. Everything else is a formula: quadrature weights, Lebedev sphere weights, Henyey-Greenstein phase functions, similarity scaling, the diffusion extrapolation length.
    • 
    
  • Not shown. One scene, one medium (g = 0.5, no absorption), my own path tracer as the judge. The network knows the hull is a unit sphere. Nothing here says it transfers to another shape without another authoring round.
    • 
    
  • Baked for speed. Only the phase functions depend on the camera. The compiler finds that split by itself, bakes the rest into two 3D textures each time the sun moves, and leaves a few lines of arithmetic per ray-march step.
